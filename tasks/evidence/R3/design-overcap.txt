0. Facts the design rests on (all from the code)
   a. Three places already own the handle of a cold shard: the open under es.shardMu (pinForRead -> openColdReadOnlyLocked), the
      check-in under es.shardMu (checkinReadStore: closes a read's handle when no pin is left and openColdShards > cap), and the
      idle tick (closeIdleShards, then enforceColdShardCap: closes the least recently used unpinned handles past the cap).
   b. ts.openColdShards counts every cold handle: retained read-only ones, read handles in flight, a late write's writable
      handle (84.5 MiB live, until the idle close) and a demoted shard's handle until the sweep closes it. Only the read door
      asks the cap; the write and demotion doors count without asking.
   c. A fan-out read visits its cold shards one after the other (queryEventShards: cold indices run in a plain loop), so one
      query holds one cold handle at a time. Several cold handles are in flight only when several queries run at once.
   d. The transient form lives longer than one read already at cap 0: readers that overlap share it. Every read door answers the
      same through it (TestColdOpen_ReadDoorsAnswerTheSameThroughATransientHandle). What it costs while it lives: the configured
      block cache cut to 16 MiB (a cost for bulk re-reads with Snappy, the default, where a missed block is decompressed again;
      little with NoCompression, where a block is a slice of the mapped table; not measured), IDSet.Has 0.1 us against 0.05 us, each label's map read from its keys at the
      first read that asks (once, the same work an eager open does). It is cheap to keep, but it is not the configured handle.
   e. checkoutStoreIfOpen (clears readTransientOpen, so a read's handle stays to the idle close) has no caller outside
      history_routing_test.go.

1. Decisions
   D1 Who decides "kept or not": checkinReadStore, under es.shardMu, as now. Only there are the pins and the cap known. The open
      does not decide; it decides which form to open, and it records that with the handle.
   D2 When the form is decided: at the open, under es.shardMu, from one read of the count: room = cap > 0 and
      openColdShards < cap. Room -> full config; no room, or cap 0 -> transient config. coldReadCfg takes the answer as a
      parameter (a config builder that reads a live counter hides the decision from the lifecycle file).
   D3 The form binds the fate: a transient handle is never kept, a full read handle is kept while the cap has room. One state
      bit, es.coldCounted, already says it: a transient handle is installed uncounted (it is outside the cap, as every cap-0
      handle is), check-in keeps a handle only if it is counted and the count is at most the cap. No new flag.
      Why not "kept whenever the count allows" (the form only a guess): the count at check-in includes handles that are certain
      to close, so the guess goes wrong in ordinary runs, not only in a rare window (section 3, W1 and W2).
   D4 The count means "handles the cap governs": retained read handles and writable cold handles. Handles in flight past the cap
      are not in it. Retention stays hard-bounded (kept <= cap); the handles in flight were never bounded by the cap.
      enforceColdShardCap lists counted handles only, so a tick does not evict a retained handle for one that is about to close.
   D5 Races, each costs allocation, none an answer:
      - cap fills between a full open and its check-in (two queries see the same free slot; a late write or a demotion counts
        without asking): the later check-in finds count > cap and closes a full handle. One full open wasted (2.0 MiB at
        ai-soc's configuration, 7.06 at the default) per collision. Accepted; no reservation. A reservation (count before the
        open, undo on failure) would thread a pre-count through setStoreLocked and the writable fallback of
        openColdReadOnlyLocked for a collision that fact c limits to concurrent queries on distinct closed days with a slot free.
      - cap frees between a transient open and its check-in (idle close of a retained day): the transient handle closes all the
        same, the next read of that day finds room and opens full. Cost: one transient open more (1.1 MiB), once.
   D6 A transient handle is never kept on purpose. Keeping it would save that one open and leave a retained handle that is not
      the configured one for as long as it is read (fact d): 16 MiB of block cache where 64 or 256 was configured, and the
      measure.txt H figure "+1.3 to +1.7 MiB live per retained handle" no longer describes every retained handle.
   D7 No eviction at the open. It needs the shard list (ts.mu; pinForRead is reached with ts.mu held, the comment in pinForRead)
      and a close of a second shard from the read path, which is a second cleanup path. It is also worse for the consumer's
      pattern: a fan-out visits N cold days in turn, so with LRU eviction every visit past the cap opens full and evicts
      (N x 2.0 MiB a query), against (N - cap) x 1.1 MiB with no eviction; three days interleaved at cap 2 would open on
      every read (30 opens for 30 reads, 12 now).
   D8 Ranking: not in this item. First come keeps the first cap days reached until they idle out; a day past the cap that is
      read far more than a retained one pays 1.1 MiB a read with no remedy. If ai-soc shows that pattern, the place is the idle
      tick: enforceColdShardCap closes the retained handle with the fewest accesses since the last tick when a closed day had
      more cold opens since the last tick (EventShard.accesses and coldOpens exist); the day's next read finds room. At most
      one swap a tick, one full open each. The number that decides: cold opens per tick on days past the cap at ai-soc. Ask
      their agent before building.
   D9 One path: the open, the check-in and the tick stay the three owners; D2-D4 change one condition in each
      (openColdReadOnlyLocked: choose the form, install uncounted; checkinReadStore: "counted and count <= cap";
      enforceColdShardCap: list counted handles). No new close site, no new goroutine, lock order unchanged (es.shardMu alone
      at open and check-in; the tick takes ts.mu.RLock then es.shardMu, as now). checkoutStoreIfOpen either goes (fact e) or
      calls countColdStoreLocked when it makes a read's handle stay.

3. b2b1e137 against the design
   The commit is one condition: coldReadCfg returns the full config when cap > 0 and openColdShards.Load() < cap, else the
   transient one. Check-in, the count and the tick are untouched; its comment accepts "a shard that closes before check-in may
   leave the transient handle kept".
   Matches: D1 (check-in decides), D2's rule and its place under es.shardMu, D5 without a reservation, D7, D8 (no eviction, no
   ranking), no second cleanup path. The measured gain in section 2 is the commit's.
   Differs, ranked:
   W1 (wrong, from the code) A full handle is closed and a transient one kept when two queries overlap. Cap 2, day A retained
      (count 1). Query 1 opens day C: 1 < 2, full, count 2. Query 2 opens day D: transient, count 3. Query 1 checks in first:
      3 > 2, C's full handle closes. Query 2 checks in: 2 <= 2, D is kept in the transient form. The full open is wasted and
      the retained handle is the cheap form. Design: D is not counted, C is kept, D closes. Change (D3, D4).
   W2 (wrong, from the code) The idle tick evicts a retained day for a handle that was opened to close. A, B retained, a read of
      day C in flight (transient, count 3) when the tick runs: enforceColdShardCap sees 3 > 2 and closes the least recently
      used unpinned handle, A or B; C's check-in then finds 2 <= 2 and keeps the transient handle. The tick runs every
      IdleTimeout / 2 and the chance it meets C in flight grows with the read load on C, so under a scan the retained set
      drifts to transient-form handles. The eviction itself predates the commit (it then left a full handle); the commit
      makes its result the cheap form. Design: the tick lists counted handles only. Change (D4).
   W3 (differs) "Kept transient" is called a rare window ("a shard that closes before check-in"); W1 and W2 make it ordinary
      under concurrent queries and under a scan at tick time. The design never keeps one (D6). Change.
   W4 (differs, small) The decision is an atomic read inside a config builder in tieredstore.go, away from the handle states.
      Design: openColdReadOnlyLocked reads the count and passes the answer. Change with W1 (AGENTS.md shard lifecycle: states and
      who moves them together).
   W5 (same in both, keep) A full handle that a late write or a demotion pushes past the cap closes at check-in: 2.0 MiB once.
   Second path: none added. The commit adds a second decision point for the handle's form that the check-in does not know of;
   that is W1-W3, not a second cleanup path.
   Kept as it is: the transient config itself, the rule "room = count < cap", no reservation, no eviction at the open.

   T5 TestOverCap_WritableHandlesTakeSlots: cap 1, a late write's handle open on day A: a read of day B opens lazy and closes;

   Mutations to run against them: check-in keeps an uncounted handle (T1, T3); the tick lists uncounted handles (T2); room is

   16 MiB cache, lazy maps, same NodeIDsByLabel answer, closed at check-in), and it catches < against <=. It does not cover
